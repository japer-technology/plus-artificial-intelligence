# The +AI Declaration

AI is already in the room.

It helps us write, think, analyse, design, code, research, calculate, create and decide.

Pretending otherwise is becoming absurd.

But there is a bigger problem than whether AI was used.

## Who takes responsibility for the result?

An AI can generate an answer.

It cannot take responsibility for sending it.

An AI can write the code.

It cannot take responsibility for deploying it.

An AI can produce the report, recommendation, image, argument or design.

It cannot stand behind the decision to publish it.

**A human can.**

That is what `+AI` means.

# **AI helped. I take responsibility.**

When I place `+AI` after my name, I am making a declaration.

I am saying that artificial intelligence materially assisted this work.

I am not hiding that fact.

I am not pretending that every word, idea or expression originated entirely in my own unaided mind.

And I am not pointing at the machine if something goes wrong.

I chose to use AI.

I chose what to accept.

I chose what to reject.

I chose what to release.

And I take responsibility for that choice.

# **Eric Mourant +AI**

`+AI` is not an apology for using artificial intelligence.

It is not a warning label.

It is not a claim that the work is perfect.

It is not a certificate that every fact has been independently verified.

It is not a declaration that AI wrote everything.

It is a declaration of something more important:

# **Ownership.**

AI may have helped produce the work.

The responsibility does not belong to AI.

It belongs to the person or organisation putting their name on the result.

For centuries, a signature has meant:

**I stand behind this.**

In an age of artificial intelligence, we need one additional piece of information:

**AI helped me get here.**

That is the purpose of `+AI`.

Three characters.

One clear admission.

# **AI helped. I take responsibility.**

The distinction between “human-made” and “AI-made” is already breaking down.

That distinction will become less useful every year.

A paragraph may begin with a human and be rewritten by AI.

A design may begin with AI and be transformed by a human.

Code may move repeatedly between human reasoning and machine generation.

Research may involve dozens of interactions between people, models, databases and software.

Trying to assign every sentence, pixel or line of code to either **human** or **AI** will become increasingly artificial.

The better question is:

# **Who is willing to put their name on the result?**

That is the line that matters.

`+AI` draws it.

Use AI.

Use it aggressively.

Use it creatively.

Use it to challenge your assumptions.

Use it to move faster.

Use it to see what you could not see alone.

But when you publish the result, do not hide behind the machine.

Put your name on it.

Take responsibility.

And if AI materially helped:
