# The plan

The central objective is **not to make the website, logo, essay or hashtag viral**.

It is to make millions of people perform one tiny, visible act:

# **Name +AI**

Every decision should optimise for that behaviour.

# 1. Capture the cultural opening

The timing is unusually strong.

In 2026, public awareness of AI is nearly universal in the United States, but concern is also high: 63% say AI is advancing too quickly, and around six in ten lack confidence that companies are developing and using it responsibly. Meanwhile, large majorities want to know when content involves AI, despite lacking confidence that they can reliably identify it. ([Pew Research Center][2])

Platforms and institutions are already responding:

* Microsoft recommends AI-assisted work carry a byline identifying the accountable human.
* The Linux kernel separates human `Signed-off-by` responsibility from AI `Assisted-by` attribution.
* Meta is expanding AI labels while explicitly warning that a proliferation of confusing disclosures could overwhelm people.
* HGCP explored structured declarations of human responsibility, although its IETF Internet-Draft is now inactive. ([Microsoft Support][3])

That creates the opening:

> **Everyone is building labels that tell us AI was present.
> `+AI` tells us which human owns the result.**

That is your category.

Do not position it as another AI-content label.

Position it as:

# **The human responsibility mark for the AI age.**

# 2. Stake the novelty claim precisely

Do not say:

> Eric Mourant invented the notation `+AI`.

Do not say:

> Eric Mourant was the first person to put `+AI` after a name.

There is earlier documented use of `Author Name + AI` as an AI-disclosure label. 

Your defensible contribution is much more interesting:

> **No earlier proposal has been identified that defines `+AI` specifically as a situational responsibility post-nominal: a mark appended to the accountable party’s name to declare that AI materially assisted the associated work and that the named party has adopted and accepts responsibility for its release.**

The original contribution is the transformation of:

**AI disclosure → accountable human declaration**

and:

**disclosure label → situational responsibility post-nominal**. 

Publish the prior art openly.

# 4. Engineer seven viral loops

Virality cannot be guaranteed. What can be engineered is a set of loops that repeatedly expose, explain and reproduce the behaviour.

## The signature loop

Every email carrying:

**Eric Mourant +AI**

places the mark in front of another person.

It does not rely on an algorithm. It travels through ordinary communication.

## The curiosity loop

A recipient sees the unfamiliar post-nominal and asks:

> What does `+AI` mean?

The answer is six words:

> **AI helped. I take responsibility.**

## The imitation loop

The notation can be copied in seconds. No app, image, registration or technical knowledge is required.

## The identity loop

People are not merely sharing your campaign.

They are temporarily modifying **their own names**:

**Jane Smith +AI**

That is much stronger than liking or reposting something.

## The artifact loop

The mark travels with the actual work:

* emails;
* documents;
* reports;
* articles;
* code;
* designs;
* images;
* presentations;
* research;
* videos;
* proposals.

## The recognition loop

You publicly recognise adopters by adding them to a voluntary **Founding Signatories** page and reposting genuine examples.

## The institutional loop

Once organisations recommend it in one bounded context, every employee, contributor or author produces more public examples.

# 5. Recruit the Founding 100 before the launch

Do not launch into an empty room.

Privately recruit a minimum of **30 serious users**, with a target of **100**, who agree to do something concrete:

> Sign one genuine AI-assisted artifact with `+AI` during launch week.

They are not endorsing an abstract philosophy. They are demonstrating the behaviour.

The founding cohort should include roughly:

* developers and open-source maintainers;
* writers, editors and journalists;
* founders and executives;
* researchers and academics;
* lawyers and policy specialists;
* teachers and educational technologists;
* designers and artists;
* consultants and engineers;
* recognised AI practitioners.

Each person receives:

1. the one-sentence definition;
2. usage examples;
3. the Declaration;
4. the FAQ;
5. a personal social card;
6. the date and launch sequence;
7. a request to use it on one real artifact.

Do not make all 100 post identical wording at the same minute. That will look manufactured.

Stagger them across 72 hours, and ask each person to explain—in their own words—why they are prepared to use the mark.

# 13. Ask institutions for bounded pilots—not endorsements

Do not initially ask a university, publisher or company to “adopt the global standard.”

Ask for a small experiment:

> For 30 days, allow contributors to use `+AI` on one category of AI-assisted work and report whether it improves disclosure and review behaviour.

Strong pilot environments include:

* software repositories;
* newsletters;
* consulting reports;
* creative studios;
* university assignments;
* research drafts;
* internal strategy documents;
* AI-assisted journalism;
* agency deliverables.

Microsoft’s accountable byline model and the Linux kernel’s separation of human sign-off from AI assistance show that these institutions are already solving adjacent problems. Your pitch is that `+AI` offers a portable human-facing shorthand rather than replacing their existing controls. ([Microsoft Support][3])

Every successful pilot becomes a case study:

> **What happened when 40 developers signed their AI-assisted work `+AI` for one month?**

That is much stronger than another opinion essay.

# 17. Create controversy without becoming hostile

The campaign should be provocative, but never accusatory toward individuals.

Strong questions:

> **Would you put `+AI` after your name?**

> **If you would not take responsibility for it, why are you publishing it?**

> **Does AI assistance weaken authorship—or make honest authorship more important?**

> **Can you claim credit for AI-assisted success while blaming AI for failure?**

> **When an AI agent acts for you, where does its authority end and yours begin?**

Do not call non-users dishonest.

Do not turn `+AI` into a moral purity badge.

The mark should invite responsibility, not divide people into virtuous and corrupt camps.

# 18. Measure adoption, not attention

The primary metric is not impressions.

It is:

# **Distinct people and organisations using `+AI` on real work.**

Track, on an aggregate and privacy-respecting basis:

* voluntary declarations;
* publicly observable uses;
* repeat users after 30 days;
* real artifacts carrying the mark;
* organisations running pilots;
* repositories using the notation;
* integrations and templates created;
* independent articles explaining it;
* translations;
* inbound references;
* return usage.

Useful thresholds:

| Milestone                       | Meaning                                     |
| ------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- |
| 100 genuine signatories         | The idea is reproducible                    |
| 1,000 distinct users            | A recognisable micro-convention exists      |
| 10 organisational pilots        | Institutional applicability is demonstrated |
| 10,000 repeat uses              | The notation is moving beyond novelty       |
| One native platform integration | A path toward infrastructure has opened     |

Avoid covert tracking, fingerprinting or mandatory accounts.

Allow people to register their first-declaration date voluntarily.

# 19. Do not make these mistakes

## Do not lead with the specification

The public gets:

> **AI helped. I take responsibility.**

Standards specialists get the specification.

## Do not launch the notation family

No `+ai`, `AI+`, `+AI!` or verification symbols yet.

One mark must become understood before extensions can carry meaning.

## Do not claim ownership of the characters

Claim the clearly defined responsibility post-nominal.

Publish the novelty analysis.

## Do not make JAPER the hero

Use:

> **Originated by Eric Mourant. Maintained openly by the +AI project.**

Not:

> A proprietary JAPER Technology product.

## Do not require registration

Registration must be optional recognition, never permission.

## Do not call it certification

Plain `+AI` is a declaration, not external verification.

## Do not flood social media with synthetic campaign spam

A movement about human responsibility cannot look like 5,000 near-identical AI-generated posts.

Fewer genuine voices are more powerful.

## Do not spend heavily on advertising before organic adoption

Money should initially go into:

* exceptional design;
* the film;
* the website;
* outreach;
* community support;
* documentation;
* tools.

Paid impressions cannot manufacture a social convention.
