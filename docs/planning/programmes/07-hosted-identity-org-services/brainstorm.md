# Brainstorm — Programme 07: Hosted Identity and Organisation Services

> Companion to [`07-hosted-identity-org-services.md`](../07-hosted-identity-org-services.md). Every member suggestion is decomposed here into **definitive features** and **pre-implementation decisions**; each has one agent request in [`requests/`](requests/). Workflow: open a request, record your choices in its §1, then give the file to your agent.

## Programme-level decisions (affect more than one request)

1. **No-account-primary-path invariant.** The free no-account path from Programmes 2–3 remains the documented primary path for issuing and keeping declarations, and no hosted surface here may become a prerequisite for declaring, verifying, or reading. This is the account-gravity guard (IMPLEMENTATION-PLAN §11.2); it is enforced in every request's §3 must-nots, not left to prose. No member may add a "sign in to check" step.
2. **Per-service launch gates (Programme 8 discipline).** Every one of the seven services ships only with, in its `docs/services/<slug>.md`: (a) the static-first failure-mode statement (service stops → previously published records stay readable, every holder can export, the free no-account path stays complete, shutdown notice period stated), (b) a threat model, (c) a privacy analysis, (d) a per-record cost model. This is IMPLEMENTATION-PLAN §11.6 applied uniformly; surfaced in each request's §2/§5 and in the spec's own §5.2 items (workspaces §5.2.12–13, exports §5.2.11–12, workflow §5.2.6/§5.2.9).
3. **Recovery design without identity docs.** All three hosted account surfaces (profiles, subdomains, workspaces) recover via codes/control proofs, never identity documents or biometrics. The "lost everything" weak spot (programme risk "Recovery design") is resolved per-member and honestly: hosted-profiles D1 fixes the lost-all-factors fallback; subdomain lapse (D1) and workspace export/offboarding (§5.2.5–6) inherit the same "records survive, control does not" principle. No member may quietly introduce identity verification.
4. **§11 cross-cutting placement.** The SSO/SCIM "identity integrations" watchlist assessment (published before the spec ships) is owned by organisation-workspaces D1. hosted-profiles D2 and workflow-automation-services' identity-provider-links note inherit that gate; neither may ship an IdP link before the assessment exists, and none may be biometric. The hosted-service gate discipline (§11.6) is programme-level decision 2. No other §11 open questions fall in Programme 7.
5. **Multi-language standard folding.** The draft standard ([`best-practice-multi-language.md`](../../../analysis/best-practice-multi-language.md), rules R1–R16, tiers T0–T4) governs every localised surface here. Five requests carry the §6 read-reference: hosted-profiles (public-page chrome R1/R4/R12/R13; profile content T4), organisation-profiles-and-policies (chrome R1/R4/R12/R13; policy documents T1 reviewed-only), named-subdomains-and-identity-bundles (resolution-page chrome R1/R4/R12/R13; reserved terms T0 never translated), approved-tool-registers (public-snapshot chrome R1/R4/R12/R13; register content T4), organisation-workspaces (private admin/manage UI chrome R1/R4/R12/R13; member/register content T4). evidence-and-compliance-exports and workflow-automation-services carry no localised surface at launch (an offline data artifact and an automation/API layer respectively); their user-facing copy stays English under R1 (source of truth), and a localised view is a later enhancement, not a launch requirement.
6. **Output locations.** Hosted-service design docs, schemas, and validators follow the repo's static-first layout (IMPLEMENTATION-PLAN §3): `docs/services/<slug>.md` for each service's design + Programme 8 gates; `docs/services/*-schema.json` plus `scripts/validate-*.mjs` / `scripts/verify-*.mjs` where the spec demands a free-floor offline artifact (approved-tool-registers schema/validator, evidence-export bundle verifier); `site/reserved-subdomain-terms.json` for the published reserved-term list. Hosted services are optional layers *around* the commons and never add files the commons depends on.

No Order-line/Sequencing tension exists in this programme: every mini-plan "Order" line and the Sequencing list agree (hosted-profiles → organisation-profiles-and-policies → approved-tool-registers schema early → organisation-workspaces → named-subdomains parallel with 3–4 → evidence-and-compliance-exports → workflow-automation-services).

## Member-by-member brainstorm

### hosted-profiles — Hosted Profiles
- **Request:** [`requests/hosted-profiles.md`](requests/hosted-profiles.md)
- **Definitive features:** hosted profile service (public view + manage view) as a presentation object over owned records, defining no new record type (§5.3); content model of optional display name/pseudonym, short context, practice-register references, declaration references, and a contact route — each with its own visibility flag and edit-revision pointer; append-only public edit history (typography correctable, meaning changes always a new revision); export-then-remove departure (full export in portable formats, free, no justification; declarations preserved per lifecycle); account recovery via codes/control proofs, never identity documents or biometrics; free basic tier defaulting to unlisted with searchability only on separate consent; claim-type labelling so a profile-level practice entry never implies use on every artifact; per-declaration status history (never merged into a profile verdict); evidence-label vocabulary with no trust score/verdict; private account data (auth/recovery/billing) stored and served separately from public records; moderation/appeal applicability with removal never deleting the holder's own declarations; no auto-created registry entry.
- **Decisions:** D1 — Recovery fallback (all factors lost); D2 — Identity-provider (SSO/IdP) links at launch; D3 — Private-account-data retention after depublishing.
- **Open questions / risks:** lost-all-factors fallback, SSO timing, and post-depublish retention are the three open items (D1–D3). Risk: **account gravity** — the profile being pressured into a "sign in to check" prerequisite; guarded by the no-account-primary-path invariant (programme-level decision 1) and §3. Because profiles are the account primitive everything else builds on, D1's honest fallback and D2's SSO deferral ripple into workspaces and subdomains.

### organisation-profiles-and-policies — Organisation Profiles and Policies
- **Request:** [`requests/organisation-profiles-and-policies.md`](requests/organisation-profiles-and-policies.md)
- **Definitive features:** public organisation profile + manage view under mandatory visible non-endorsement text; content model of organisation name and declared context, versioned policy reference, representatives (role/dates/authority evidence), disclosed teams and contacts (each consented), approved-tool register reference, and declaration references — each with its own visibility flag and revision pointer; versioned, dated, change-noted policy-revision flow (never in-place edits; references the spec version it aligns with); role/representative change events (offboarded representatives lose edit as of the event; historical edits stay attributed); domain-control evidence display (a claimed domain never rendered as controlled without a control proof); member-consent listing and withdrawal (a person's own declarations never owned by the organisation); human `responsibleParty` on every listed declaration (organisation never a sole signer); four-way claim-type separation; no trust score/rank/verdict.
- **Decisions:** D1 — Domain-control proof: precondition vs stronger label; D2 — Non-endorsement wording at launch; D3 — Policy-document translation tier.
- **Open questions / risks:** domain-proof precondition and exact non-endorsement wording are the two spec §10 items (D1–D2; wording needs legal review); policy-text translation tier is D3. Risk: the profile drifting into certification/reputation — guarded by the mandatory non-endorsement text, the human-signer rule, and the no-score must-nots.

### approved-tool-registers — Approved-Tool Registers
- **Request:** [`requests/approved-tool-registers.md`](requests/approved-tool-registers.md)
- **Definitive features:** approval-register JSON schema (§5.3) usable offline and `file://`-safe with no account; each approval entry recording subject (tool-taxonomy identifier where one exists), permitted purposes, data rules, owner, review date, `active|retired` status, `authorisedBy`/`authorisedAt`, and the `approved` claim with a policy reference — never `used-on-artifact`; first-class exceptions (identifier, description, owner, expiry/review date, justification category, never silently extended) and acknowledgements (party, policy version, date, never read as use); organisation-only default with role-based authoring inherited from organisation workspaces; redacted dated public snapshot (omits security-posture fields, labels every field self-declared); past-due review detection via machine-comparable review dates; retired policies retained as history (retirement is a status change, never deletion); no ranking or endorsement of approved tools.
- **Decisions:** D1 — Retention schedule for entries and acknowledgements; D2 — Embed vs reference policy text.
- **Open questions / risks:** retention schedule and embed-vs-reference are the two spec §10 items (D1–D2). Risk: the "approved" label reading as use or as endorsement — guarded by the never-`used-on-artifact` rule, the org-only default, and the no-ranking must-not. The schema is free-floor-readable, so it must be authored/readable offline before any hosted authoring exists.

### named-subdomains-and-identity-bundles — Named Subdomains and Identity Bundles
- **Request:** [`requests/named-subdomains-and-identity-bundles.md`](requests/named-subdomains-and-identity-bundles.md)
- **Definitive features:** subdomain claim/resolve/manage/lapse flow resolving a claimed label to a canonical ID-based target (declaration or profile), never a new identity; published, versioned allocation rule plus a reserved-term list (project name, `+AI` mark, core governance/continuity terms, free message-bearing labels); IDN normalisation (NFC) with homograph flagging/rejection; time-limited claims with renewal, grace, then revert-to-tombstone or release, the underlying target always intact at its canonical ID; transfer-as-event (a recorded change of holder carrying no claim about the prior holder); abuse handling with suspension, a published appeal route, and removal on upheld abuse while preserving the holder's records; the address-not-identity label on every response; email aliases as mailbox-filtering conveniences (plus-addressing), never identity proof; operational-cost pricing with the no-legitimacy statement.
- **Decisions:** D1 — Grace period and release schedule for lapsed names; D2 — Mailbox control proof at claim.
- **Open questions / risks:** grace-period/release durations and mailbox-proof-at-claim are the two spec §10 items (D1–D2). Risk: the address being read as identity/rank — guarded by the always-shown address-not-identity label, the no-identity-proof-to-claim rule, and the never-repurpose invariant. The free message-bearing variant (Programme 6) defines the shared reserved-label rules first.

### organisation-workspaces — Organisation Workspaces
- **Request:** [`requests/organisation-workspaces.md`](requests/organisation-workspaces.md)
- **Definitive features:** workspace service with absolute tenant isolation (demonstrated in the threat model), server-side roles/permissions with immediate effect and session revocation, approval queues, and a consolidated append-only audit log (actor, action, date, exportable); free exports of records, registers, and consolidated history in portable formats; single-action offboarding (immediate access removal, past actions attributed, personal declarations never owned); legal holds via declaration-lifecycle events; SSO/SCIM optional, assessment-gated, and non-biometric; published static-first failure-mode statement and demonstrated degraded mode; privacy analysis and threat model plus real-organisation pilot evidence before building as a paid service; the free no-account path covering every core capability (declaration, practice register, approved-tool register, policy acknowledgement).
- **Decisions:** D1 — SSO/SCIM assessment and launch scope; D2 — Initial per-role permission set; D3 — Ship before vs after managed hosting.
- **Open questions / risks:** SSO/SCIM timing, the exact role permission set, and managed-hosting sequencing are the three open items (D1–D3). Risk: **tenant isolation** is an absolute requirement (programme risk) — it must be demonstrated in the threat model, not asserted; budget security review before pilots. Risk: workspaces becoming a declaration-validity prerequisite — guarded by the signer-acceptance rule and the free-alternative must-nots.

### evidence-and-compliance-exports — Evidence and Compliance Exports
- **Request:** [`requests/evidence-and-compliance-exports.md`](requests/evidence-and-compliance-exports.md)
- **Definitive features:** bundle-assembly service enforcing a required scope statement (parties, artifacts/scopes, date range, record classes) that the bundle must never exceed; signed, hash-pinned manifest listing each item's canonical identifier, `sha256` hash, source snapshot version, and evidence-label vocabulary; offline verification with no account; explicit provenance (declarationId, dated event sequence, control-evidence labels at issuance and last check); visibility filtering and redaction applied at assembly (local records never included, unlisted/organisation-only excluded from public bundles, field-level redaction leaving explicit `[redacted]` markers); expiry/staleness labelling with scheduled re-issue; access control for non-public bundles via organisation roles with recorded grants; optional professional review (criteria-published, reviewer-dated, never certification); standing no-certification warning; payment buying scheduling/packaging/access only.
- **Decisions:** D1 — Scheduled bundles: on-demand vs recurring at launch; D2 — Default expiry window per record class.
- **Open questions / risks:** on-demand-vs-recurring and expiry windows are the two spec §10 items (D1–D2; expiry follows proofs-of-control freshness windows). Risk: the bundle being read as certification — guarded by the standing warning, the no-verdict rule, and the immutable versioned-bundle design. The bundle is an offline data artifact, so it carries no localised chrome at launch (programme-level decision 5).

### workflow-automation-services — Workflow Automation Services
- **Request:** [`requests/workflow-automation-services.md`](requests/workflow-automation-services.md)
- **Definitive features:** workflow service with least-privilege server-side permissions, bounded idempotent retries (backoff, idempotency keys, visible failure state), and an append-only audit log; customer-controlled deployment (every workflow runnable on the customer's own infrastructure via the commons); signed, rate-limited webhooks; connectors, bulk operations, scheduled validation, review reminders, and identity-provider links (assessment-gated, non-biometric); every offered workflow citing pilot evidence; no consequential decision automated (a human reviewer always remains) and the manual free path always performing the same action; explicit maintenance ownership with unmaintained workflows flagged or disabled with notice; published privacy analysis, threat model, and static-first failure-mode statement.
- **Decisions:** D1 — Paid vs free workflow boundary; D2 — Retry and backoff defaults per workflow class.
- **Open questions / risks:** paid-vs-free boundary and retry/backoff defaults are the two spec §10 items (D1–D2). Risk: automation changing outcomes or locking customers in — guarded by the no-consequential-decision rule, the always-manual-free-path rule, and customer-controlled deployment. Identity-provider links inherit the SSO/SCIM assessment gate owned by organisation-workspaces D1 (a §2 note, not a decision here).
